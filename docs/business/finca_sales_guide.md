# Sales Playbook: Upselling FiNCA to FiLTER
**Target**: FiNCA to FiLTER (Old Fourth Ward & Side Saddle)  
**Objective**: Transition from Free Tier to Promoter/Headliner Tier  
**Prepared for**: Jacob  

---

## 🔍 Analysis of Jacob's Approach

Jacob’s instincts are solid on two fronts:
1. **"Getting their story"**: This is an excellent, low-friction way to build rapport. Coffee shops thrive on community, identity, and storytelling.
2. **"Keeping it casual"**: Pitching a queer-owned neighborhood coffee shop requires a relational, collaborative tone—not a high-pressure corporate pitch.

### ⚠️ The Trap: "I've done all I can for free"
This phrase is a classic sales bottleneck. It frames the conversation around **Jacob’s labor** rather than **Finca's business growth**. It sounds apologetic, transactional, and slightly defensive. 

* **The Shift**: Instead of framing the upgrade as *"I can't do any more free work,"* frame it as *"We have built a platform that is actively directing World Cup tourists and local queer folks to businesses like yours. Let's make sure your business is positioned to capture 100% of that traffic."*

---

## 💬 The Conversation Flow & Scripts

### Phase 1: The "Story" & Rapport Builder (Low Pressure)
Start with their story. It makes them feel valued and shows we care about their brand.

> **Jacob**: *"Hey [Owner Name], I love what you guys have done with the space. I actually wanted to grab your official 'origin story'—how you got started with radical hospitality and sourcing from minority roasters. We want to add a beautiful write-up to your OutATL profile to make sure people really get the vibe before they even walk in."*

### Phase 2: The Value Hook (Show, Don't Tell)
Show them the active platform and explain *why* having a complete profile matters right now.

> **Jacob**: *"Right now, we have your basic location details live. With the World Cup kicking off and tourists looking for safe, queer-owned spaces to get their morning coffee or do some remote work, we’re seeing a huge surge in directory traffic.*
>
> *To really capture those folks, we want to unlock your full profile. That means putting your website link directly on the page, linking your Instagram, and listing your weekly events—like your Queer Trivia nights—so people know exactly when to visit."*

### Phase 3: The Pitch & Scarcity (The World Cup Angle)
Bridge to the investment using the World Cup momentum.

> **Jacob**: *"Normally, our Promoter tier is [Standard Price]/month. But because we're in the middle of the World Cup rush and want our anchor local spots looking their best, we’re doing a special tournament rate of **[Promo Price]/month** that locks you in through the finals. It basically pays for itself if it brings in just 3 or 4 new daily regulars."*

---

## 🛡️ Objection Busters

### Objection 1: "We don't have the budget right now."
* **The Core Fear**: Unsure of the Return on Investment (ROI).
* **The Reframe**: Frame the cost against the value of a single customer.
* **Script**: 
  > *"I completely get it. Budgets are tight. But think of it this way: the Promoter tier is about the cost of two lattes a week. If having your Instagram, website, and Queer Trivia events listed brings in just two new customers a week who buy a coffee and a pastry, the listing has completely paid for itself. The rest is pure profit from the World Cup crowd."*

### Objection 2: "Can't we just stay on the free tier?"
* **The Core Fear**: "Good enough" syndrome.
* **The Reframe**: Highlight the friction they are causing for their own customers.
* **Script**: 
  > *"You absolutely can stay on the free tier, and we'll keep your location details up. The challenge is that today’s customers are impatient. If a tourist finds you on OutATL but can't click directly to your Instagram to see your pastry selection, or can't click to your website to check your bean origin, they will often just click on the next coffee shop that has those quick links. We want to remove all friction so they choose you."*

### Objection 3: "We already get enough foot traffic / We do our own marketing."
* **The Core Fear**: "We don't need help."
* **The Reframe**: Focus on the *specific* niche audience OutATL owns.
* **Script**: 
  > *"That's awesome, and it shows—you guys are always buzzing. But the audience we're bringing in is highly specific: LGBTQ+ travelers and locals who are actively looking to support queer-owned businesses during the World Cup. They aren't just looking for coffee; they are looking for community. OutATL is the bridge that tells them 'this is your spot.'"*

---

## 💡 Confidence-Building Tips for Jacob

1. **You are a Partner, not a Peddler**: Jacob isn't begging for money; he is offering a premium advertising slot on a highly targeted directory during the biggest sporting event in the city's history. He is helping Finca win.
2. **Use the Mobile View**: Tell Jacob to open the OutATL directory on his phone right in front of the owner. Show them a Promoter/Headliner listing (with gorgeous photos, social links, and events) and then show them Finca's current free listing. The visual contrast and the "Upgrade to Unlock" callout do 80% of the selling.
3. **Assume the "Yes"**: Talk about the photo shoot and event listings as if they are definitely going to happen. *"Once we get this upgraded today, I'll get those trivia nights uploaded immediately so they're live for this Wednesday."*
